Universal Design Principles
Patterns that appear across every genre and dictate the strongest sites regardless of sector.
Core thesis: The best commerce sites reduce uncertainty at the moment it matters. Visual style follows the decision the customer needs to make — not aesthetic preference.

Search vs. Editorial Balance

Search earns space with broad catalogs (1000+ SKUs). Editorial composition earns space when taste, material, or provenance IS the product. Don't add search to feel "complete."

Useful Specificity

Ingredients, dimensions, fit, compatibility, delivery windows, lead times, renewal terms — these build trust. Decorative badges without substance destroy it.

Low-Risk Selection

Variants, comparison, guidance, guarantees, and clear stock signals matter more than visual polish. Make selection feel safe, not impressive.

Action Matches Intent

"Add to bag" ≠ "Choose size" ≠ "Schedule pickup" ≠ "Request quote." These are different customer decisions — don't disguise them behind generic CTAs.

Mobile is Structural

Tap targets, persistent cart, short forms, and clear next steps must survive narrow viewports. Design mobile-first for high-frequency genres.

Frequency Dictates Density

Daily essentials pack tight; considered purchases breathe. Product card density correlates with repeat purchase velocity, not catalog size alone.

Cross-Genre Design Patterns
Reusable patterns that span multiple genres, with guidance on when each applies.

Search vs. Navigation

Search-dominant: Grocery, Electronics, Marketplace (1000+ SKUs). Navigation-dominant: Fashion, Artisan, Luxury (200 curated pieces). Match to catalog breadth.

Product Card Density

Dense grid: Essential, Grocery, Marketplace. Tall hero cards: Fashion, Luxury. Density tracks repeat purchase velocity.

Trust Signal Type

Beauty: ingredients. Electronics: specs/warranty. Artisan: maker stories. B2B: certification. Match the signal to the risk being reduced.

Cart Visibility

Persistent mini-cart: Grocery, Food. Slide-out drawer: Essential, Fashion. Hidden until add: Luxury. Visibility tracks frequency.

Location Priority

Critical: Local, Grocery, Food. Moderate: Home (delivery). Absent: Digital goods, Subscription. Follow fulfillment complexity.

Time Dimension

Grocery: delivery slots. Food: pickup times. Artisan: lead times. Subscription: cadence. Luxury: appointment booking.

Implementation Roadmap
Three-phase rollout for any genre, from foundation to differentiation.

Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1-2)

  • Select 1-2 primary genres matching your business model and purchase behavior
  • Implement search vs. editorial navigation based on catalog size (1000+ SKUs → search)
  • Set product grid density: tight for high-frequency, spacious for considered purchases
  • Ensure mobile layout: tap targets ≥ 44px, persistent cart, thumb-friendly zones

Phase 2 — Trust & Conversion (Weeks 3-4)

  • Add genre-appropriate trust signals: specs, ingredients, maker stories, warranties
  • Include dimensions, compatibility, fit guides, or lead times on product pages
  • Choose cart pattern: persistent (high freq) → drawer (medium) → hidden (considered)
  • Streamline checkout to 2-3 steps, add express options

Phase 3 — Differentiation (Weeks 5-6)

  • Apply genre color palette and typography as visual identity
  • Add comparison tools, builders, customizers, or booking features
  • Implement social proof: reviews, certificates, case studies
  • Layer advanced filtering: facets, size guides, material selectors
Key Takeaways
Principles that apply regardless of genre, sector, or scale.
Match genre to behavior, not aesthetics. Don't force luxury spacing on grocery essentials, or dense grids on handmade goods. Purchase frequency and decision complexity drive the pattern.
Trust signals must be real. Never fabricate specs, ingredients, reviews, delivery windows, or origin claims. Useful specificity builds trust; decorative badges destroy it.
Mobile is not a breakpoint — it's a structure. Tap targets, persistent cart context, and clear next steps must survive narrow viewports. Design mobile-first for high-frequency genres.
Actions should match intent. "Add to bag," "Choose size," "Schedule pickup," "Request quote," and "Subscribe" are different customer decisions. Don't hide them behind generic CTAs.
Search vs. editorial is a spectrum, not a binary. Most sites sit between the two poles. The question is how much screen real estate each earns — and the answer follows catalog breadth and decision type.
